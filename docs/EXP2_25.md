# EXP2.25: Sync-Horizon Sweep and Measurement-Wall Curve

## Question

Two predictions from the DiLoCo-poison analysis (EXP2.23/24 and
`docs/PAPER_IDEA_DILOCO_POISON.md`):

1. Outer momentum's value is a horizon bet: as the sync interval H grows,
   merged directions persist longer and momentum should cross from harmful
   to helpful.
2. Merge-action selection sits behind a measurement wall: growing the
   anchor probe should saturate quickly, because the action gap for most
   groups lies below any affordable probe's noise floor.

## E2 Result: The Momentum Crossover

64-row eval loss (H=64 row from EXP2.23/24):

| H | μ=0 | μ=0.5 | μ=0.9 | μ=0.9 penalty vs best |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | **1.3519** | 1.3632 | 1.4383 | 0.0864 |
| 64 | **1.3578** | 1.3614 | 1.4193 | 0.0615 |
| 256 | 1.3805 | **1.3674** | 1.3977 | 0.0303 |

1. **The crossover exists and sits between H=64 and H=256.** Memoryless
   SGD wins at H ∈ {16, 64}; at H=256 μ=0.5 wins by `0.0131`.
2. **The μ=0.9 penalty decays monotonically with H** (0.086 → 0.062 →
   0.030), converging toward the DiLoCo design regime where μ=0.9 is
   established practice.
3. **Momentum is a long-horizon stabilizer:** μ=0 degrades as syncs get
   rarer (1.3519 → 1.3805) while μ=0.5 is nearly flat (1.3632 → 1.3674).

Tape geometry ties the crossover to the proposed mechanism — the same
μ=0.9 becomes better-aligned as H grows:

| Arm | Step norm | Applied-step cosine | History/current |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| h16-mu0 | 0.838 | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| h16-mu05 | 1.366 | 0.980 | 0.230 |
| h16-mu09 | 1.974 | 0.752 | 0.823 |
| h64-mu09 (EXP2.23) | 4.025 | 0.797 | 0.685 |
| h256-mu0 | 3.517 | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| h256-mu05 | 5.500 | 0.986 | 0.158 |
| h256-mu09 | 7.533 | 0.872 | 0.489 |

μ=0.9's rotation heals with H (cosine 0.752 → 0.797 → 0.872; history
dominance 0.823 → 0.685 → 0.489) and the loss penalty tracks it
monotonically. Longer windows average more data per merged update, the
merged direction becomes more persistent, and memory stops fighting fresh
evidence.

## E3 Result: The Wall Saturates

Frozen selector on the same 80-group sync capture, anchor probe size swept:

| Anchor panels | Selection rate | Gain vs ×1 | Headroom captured |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.475 | 0.000898 | 44.2% |
| 8 | 0.1625 | 0.001073 | 53.8% |
| 16 | 0.1625 | 0.001098 | 54.0% |

Going 2→8 panels prunes false selections (rate 0.475 → 0.16) and lifts
captured headroom by 9.6 points. Going 8→16 — doubling measurement cost —
adds **0.2 points**. The probe saturates: the selectable groups are the
early large-gap ones, and no affordable probe size reaches the rest,
consistent with the gap-below-noise-floor analysis (median late-training
gap 0.00084 vs per-decision SE 0.00138).

Cross-hardware numerics note: the 2/16-panel replays ran on Blackwell
(torch 2.8.0 cu128) versus A100 (torch 2.5.1 cu121) for the 8-panel run;
the recomputed baseline negative rate moved 0.3875 → 0.4125 (2/80
borderline groups flipped sign from bf16 kernel differences alone —
itself a measure of how small these one-step signals are).

## Limitations

1. Single seed (223), fixed LR 0.175 across the grid. Realized step norms
   grow with H for every μ (0.84 → 3.5 at μ=0), and μ inflates them
   further; the H=256 μ=0.5 win may include a scale component. A
   norm-matched μ=0 vs μ=0.5 pair at H=256 is the isolating follow-up.
2. H=256 has only 20 outer steps — momentum acts over few rounds; the
   crossover location may shift with budget.
3. Same one model/dataset/LoRA config as the whole series.
4. The E3 sweep reuses one capture; probe-size behavior on the H=16/H=256
   captures is future work (captures retained in S3).

## Autocorrelation Result: The Sign Inverts and the Law Sharpens

Round-to-round merged-delta direction autocorrelation ρ, computed on CPU
from the retained μ=0 captures (per-fragment consecutive commits, plain
average of the four equal-weight candidates):

| H | Pairs | mean ρ = cos(d_t, d_{t-1}) | p10 | p90 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 316 | 0.9824 | 0.970 | 0.991 |
| 64 | 76 | 0.9251 | 0.853 | 0.961 |
| 256 | 16 | 0.7339 | 0.644 | 0.793 |

Persistence **decreases** with H — the opposite of the naive "long
horizons persist more" story. The law that fits everything is momentum
amplification: on a direction with autocorrelation ρ, the buffer
amplifies the effective outer step by ≈ 1/(1 − μρ). Applying
η_eff = η/(1 − μρ) with η = 0.175 against the tuned reference 0.28:

| Arm | η_eff | Predicted | Measured |
|---|---:|---|---|
| H=16 μ=0.9 | 1.48 | severe overshoot | 1.4383, worst ✓ |
| H=64 μ=0.9 | 1.06 | strong overshoot | 1.4193 ✓ |
| H=256 μ=0.9 | 0.51 | mild overshoot | 1.3977 ✓ |
| H=64 μ=0.5 | 0.33 | ≈ tuned | ties SGD-0.28 ✓ |
| H=256 μ=0.5 | 0.28 | exactly tuned | best at H=256 ✓ |
| H=256 μ=0 | 0.175 | understep | loses to μ=0.5 ✓ |
| EXP2.24 μ=0.9 η=0.028 | 0.24–0.28 | ≈ tuned | tied best ✓ |

The "momentum crossover" is therefore an **effective-LR phenomenon**:
optimal μ at fixed η rises with H because ρ falls with H. The rotation
and oscillation seen in the tapes are the overshoot dynamics of μρ → 1,
not an independent primary cause — though EXP2.24's norm-matched arm
(right average scale, still 0.04 worse) shows a residual oscillation
penalty beyond mean amplification.

Caveats: ρ is measured on μ=0 trajectories (closed-loop ρ under momentum
differs); LoRA rank 2 constrains deltas to a low-dimensional space and
may structurally inflate ρ — the full-finetune check (E4) is required
before generalizing; H=256 has only 16 pairs.

## Correction (2026-07-12, independent review)

An independent derivation (Codex gpt-5.6-sol, xhigh; transcript in the
session scratchpad) corrected the law: for this Nesterov form the
signal-aligned amplification is η_eff = η(1 + μ/(1−μρ)), not η/(1−μρ)
(they agree only at ρ=1). Under the corrected formula, aligned gain alone
retrodicts the μ=0.9 failures and the H=256 μ=0.5 win but NOT the μ=0
wins at H∈{16,64}; those require the second term the review derived —
off-direction variance accumulation in the buffer, with RMS amplification
A²_RMS = 1 + 2μ/(1−μρ) + μ²(1+μρ)/((1−μ²)(1−μρ)) — which punishes
memory hardest at high ρ. The mechanism claim therefore becomes a
two-term law (aligned overstep + variance accumulation), the ρ input must
be the per-tensor production-RDA autocorrelation (not the plain-average
proxy used above), and the single-term retrodiction table above is
superseded. The controller's empirical results (EXP2.26) are unaffected;
its rule should be recalibrated against the corrected law.
