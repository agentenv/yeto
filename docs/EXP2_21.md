# EXP2.21: Direct Scalar Action-Probe Replay

## Question

Can a current-state anchor probe choose the outer-step magnitude for a complete
four-responder fragment group more reliably than it can choose a
leave-one-responder-out direction?

EXP2.20 showed that LOO direction selection has oracle headroom but does not
recover it. EXP2.21 keeps all four equal-token responders and changes only the
scalar applied to the exact production full-group SGD step.

## Frozen Baseline

```text
model: Qwen3.5-9B, LoRA rank 2
learners/fragments: 4/4
outer optimizer: plain SGD
outer LR: 0.28
delta correction: none
local window: 8192 tokens / 64 microsteps
delay after snapshot: 0/800/1600/2400 ms plus 50 ms jitter
schedule: strict quorum, 80 complete outer groups
```

The captures are the final seed-223 development trajectory and seed-239
confirmation trajectory from EXP2.19.

## Action Grid

The offline replay materializes the exact production full-group update once,
then applies these outer-step multipliers:

```text
0.50, 0.75, 0.875, 1.00, 1.125, 1.25, 1.50
```

Every action uses the same four candidates and production per-tensor AVG/RDA
merge. There is no HeLoCo correction and no candidate filtering. Because the
outer optimizer is memoryless SGD, scaling the sealed applied step is exact.

## Data Separation

```text
training source window: first 5,064 Capybara rows before deterministic shuffle
anchor A: 256 rows beginning at source offset 11,000
oracle E: 512 rows beginning at source offset 12,000
oracle F: 512 rows beginning at source offset 13,000
```

The three generated manifests report zero canonical overlap with training,
earlier holdouts, and one another. The action selector sees only anchor A.

The primary replay uses eight anchor panels and eight oracle panels. Each
panel owns a disjoint round-robin set of source conversations and contains two
128-token packed blocks with positive assistant target weight. Prompt-only
packed blocks are skipped rather than treated as zero-information panels.

## Frozen Selector

The predeclared selector uses multiplier `1.0` as fallback. For each other
action and panel:

```text
gain_j(action) = loss_j(multiplier=1.0) - loss_j(action)
```

An action is eligible only when:

```text
mean gain >= 0.00025
mean gain - 2.365 * SE > 0
at least 6/8 panel gains are positive
```

The eligible action with the largest lower confidence bound is selected.
Otherwise the replay commits multiplier `1.0`. Oracle E and F never affect
the decision.

## Oracle-E Result

The complete primary replay processed 160 groups, 80 per seed.

| Metric | Seed 223 | Seed 239 | Aggregate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Groups | 80 | 80 | 160 |
| Mean gain vs multiplier 1.0 | 0.001009 | 0.001034 | **0.001021** |
| Selection rate | 0.2125 | 0.1750 | 0.1938 |
| Best fixed 1.5 gain | 0.000963 | 0.000964 | 0.000964 |
| Adaptive gain vs fixed 1.5 | 0.0000457 | 0.0000701 | **0.0000579** |

The chosen multiplier distribution is:

| Multiplier | Groups |
|---:|---:|
| 0.75 | 3 |
| 1.00 | 129 |
| 1.125 | 4 |
| 1.25 | 4 |
| 1.50 | 20 |

Multipliers `0.5` and `0.875` were never selected. Removing them leaves an
exact five-action online protocol:

```text
A0 = 1.0 fallback
A1 = 0.75
A2 = 1.125
A3 = 1.25
A4 = 1.5
```

The mean oracle-best scalar headroom is `0.001821`, so the frozen selector
captures `56.1%` of scalar oracle headroom. The baseline and selected negative
merge rates are both `0.325`; the selector improves utility without reducing
the raw negative rate on oracle E.

## Why This Is Adaptive

The result is not explained by choosing multiplier `1.5` everywhere. The
fixed `1.5` action is strongly positive early and becomes harmful later:

| Seed | Steps | Frozen selector gain | Fixed 1.5 gain | Selector action rate | Fixed 1.5 negative rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 223 | 1-20 | 0.004007 | 0.004260 | 0.50 | 0.00 |
| 223 | 21-40 | -0.000023 | -0.000154 | 0.15 | 0.50 |
| 223 | 41-60 | 0.000047 | -0.000286 | 0.10 | 0.65 |
| 223 | 61-80 | 0.000005 | 0.000032 | 0.10 | 0.30 |
| 239 | 1-20 | 0.004237 | 0.004920 | 0.55 | 0.10 |
| 239 | 21-40 | -0.000003 | -0.000459 | 0.05 | 0.55 |
| 239 | 41-60 | -0.000098 | -0.000326 | 0.10 | 0.35 |
| 239 | 61-80 | 0.000000 | -0.000279 | 0.00 | 0.45 |

The probe expands the step primarily in the early trajectory and abstains as
the larger step stops transferring to the current state. This is the first
offline policy in this project that beats both multiplier `1.0` and the best
fixed scalar on both final-SGD seeds.

## Secondary Diagnostic Rules

Two rules were evaluated after reading the primary result. They are useful
mechanism diagnostics, not untouched confirmations.

### Positive-Utility Largest Step

Choose the largest scalar whose anchor utility relative to the current model
has mean at least `0.005`, one-standard-error lower bound above zero, and at
least `6/8` positive panels. Fall back to multiplier `1.0`.

```text
mean gain vs multiplier 1.0: 0.001131
selection rate:             0.250
negative-rate drop:         0.019
```

The gain is positive on both seeds, but the strict-negative rate regresses.
This rule is not the first online candidate.

### Shrink From Fixed 1.5

Use multiplier `1.5` as fallback and select a lower scalar when it beats the
fallback on at least five of eight panels with mean gain at least `0.0001`.

```text
gain vs fixed 1.5, seed 223: 0.0001166
gain vs fixed 1.5, seed 239: 0.0000722
two-seed mean:               0.0000944
strict-negative drop:        0.100
```

This is evidence that anchor probing can improve even the best fixed scalar,
but the rule was designed after inspecting the primary oracle-E result. It is
secondary until confirmed on a new endpoint or trajectory.

## Robustness Runs

Two confirmation runs were started before online training:

1. Keep the same anchor decisions and evaluate on disjoint oracle F.
2. Increase anchor panels from eight to 32 while keeping oracle E fixed.

Their final results are added below when complete. No online active run begins
until both artifacts are archived.

## Online Policy

The primary online action family is:

```text
policy: probe_lr_v1
A0: exact full-group SGD preview at multiplier 1.0
A1-A4: exact sealed previews at 0.75, 1.125, 1.25, and 1.5
selector: min_gain=0.00025, lcb_z=2.365, min_win_rate=0.75
failure behavior: commit A0
```

`probe_lr_shadow` performs the identical probe and pays the identical delay,
but always commits A0. Any timeout, stale preview, malformed response,
non-finite loss, digest mismatch, or unsafe selected action falls back to A0.

The first online stages are:

```text
S0: Qwen3.5-9B, 2 strict steps, shadow protocol smoke
S1: Qwen3.5-9B, 24 strict steps, active versus shadow on one development seed
C1: Qwen3.5-9B, 80 strict steps, frozen active versus shadow on a fresh seed
C2: repeat C1 on a second fresh seed if C1 passes
```

The primary online comparator is shadow, not an unprobed token-weighted run,
because probe delay changes asynchronous learner cadence.

## Offline Decision

The scalar action family passes the first offline utility gate:

```text
all seeds positive:                    pass
mean gain vs multiplier 1.0 >= 0.001: pass
adaptive beats fixed 1.5 on both:      pass
selection rate in [0.05, 0.50]:       pass
anchor/oracle disjointness:            pass
```

This is permission to finish the online scalar protocol and run shadow smoke.
It is not yet evidence that the policy improves a counterfactual training
trajectory.

## Known Limitations

1. These are one-step counterfactuals on trajectories generated by multiplier
   `1.0`; action choices are not propagated into learner training.
2. The same fixed anchor is reused across groups. Online confirmation must
   watch for anchor overfitting and endpoint disagreement.
3. The best fixed scalar is at the upper edge of the tested grid. A wider
   scalar screen is a diagnostic, not a substitute for online stability.
4. Only two final-SGD seeds and one model/data configuration are represented.
5. The adaptive advantage over fixed `1.5` is small; the meaningful claim
   requires active-versus-shadow online replication.
